All of this data is **synthetic**. It was written by LLM agents (Claude) and contains **no real user data**. Items are realistic `{ title, url, group }` tabs using the group ids in `src/classify/seeds.ts` (the installed groups and the 14 on-demand ones); `other` marks genuinely miscellaneous pages.
- `training/train-a.json` has 500 items, weighted towards developer, AI and movie/TV tabs. A separate agent wrote it blind, without seeing `src/` or `tests/`.
- `training/train-b.json` has 500 items modelling general users, about 20% `other`. Another separate agent wrote it blind.
- `training/train-c.json` has 814 items aimed at the confusing boundaries (programming/docs/learning/ai, news/finance/other, videos/music/movies/tv/games, …), about 18% `other`. A separate agent wrote it blind from a written labelling guide; items whose URL or title also appear in either holdout were removed before use.
- `training/train-d.json` has 70 items written by the implementer after a live test filed Malwarebytes Browser Guard and AdBlock pages under Social: security tools, VPNs, password managers, browser add-on store and install pages, and account/sign-in pages (`other`), plus security topics that do belong to a group (OWASP and MDN → docs, breach reports → news, security courses → learning).
- `tests/quality/fixture.json` has 320 items, 28 of them `other`. The classifier implementer wrote it during Task 5. It was once the dev set and is now only training data.
- `training/train-e.json` has 2,856 items for the 14 on-demand labels (hardware, science, sports, food, cars, home, fashion, pets, family, jobs, books, photo, anime, realestate; about 180–220 each), plus border rows labelled with old labels (shopping, health, learning, movies, …). Several blind agents wrote it from `training/LABELING.md`, each covering at most about four labels; 11 items whose URL or title also appear in a holdout or in `youtube-topics.json` were removed before use.
- `training/LABELING.md` is the labelling guide for new and re-labelled rows: what each label covers, the YouTube rule (a video is labelled by its subject; `videos` only for videos with no clear subject or a subject with no label) and the borders between labels. In Stage 1 a blind agent re-labelled 13 YouTube rows of the pool by it. When the 14 on-demand labels were added, blind agents re-labelled 221 rows of train-a, train-b, train-c and the fixture by its new borders (mostly `other` or `videos` rows that now have a label).
`npm run train:head` embeds the pool with `embedText` and the bundled model (bge-small-en-v1.5, mean pooling). It then trains a multinomial logistic-regression head with class-balanced weights and L2 regularisation, using full-batch Adam and a seeded split. It uses a stratified 15% validation split of the pool to choose:
- the L2 strength,

- the learned-exemplar override similarity, at ≥ 95% precision.
The head's scores are blended with a vote of the 10 nearest pool items (`knn` in head.json: int8 vectors of the whole pool, weight 0.3, chosen by 5-fold cross-validation); thresholds are chosen on the blended scores. The Unsorted probability threshold comes from 5-fold out-of-fold scores over the whole pool (a single val split was too noisy for it), maximising right − 2 × wrong, so a weak guess stays Unsorted instead of landing in a wrong group, with ≤ 30% of `other` items auto-assigned. The final head is retrained on the whole pool and written to `src/classify/head.json`, together with `poolHash` and its validation metrics.
- `tests/quality/holdout.json` has 200 items, about 14% `other`. A separate agent wrote it without seeing `src/` or `tests/`.
- It is **never** used for training or tuning. `accuracy.test.ts` prints only aggregate numbers for it.
- Do not open it to tune against it, or it stops being a holdout.
- `tests/quality/holdout2.json` has 300 items (15% `other`), tabs of eight imagined people, written by another separate agent after the old holdout had been used in past model choices. It is the honest score: never trained or tuned on. `accuracy.test.ts` also reports it without rows that share a URL or title with the training pool ("clean").
- `tests/quality/holdout3.json` has 698 items, written by another separate blind agent from `training/LABELING.md` after the 14 on-demand labels were added: 28–30 per new label, 14–19 per old label and 32 `other`. It is never trained or tuned on; `accuracy.test.ts` scores it with every group present (installed plus each on-demand group) and prints aggregates and per-label rates only.
- `tests/quality/youtube-topics.json` has 149 YouTube videos (90 about a subject, 59 `videos` with no clear subject), written blind from `training/LABELING.md`. It is never trained on. `accuracy.test.ts` runs it through the product pipeline (`pickGroup`, with its Videos fallback) and prints aggregates only.
`accuracy.test.ts` runs the real model (`public/models`, no network) through the caller pipeline: `ruleGroup`, then `classify`, then Unsorted. It prints how many tabs land in a wrong group, checks that security-tool, add-on and account pages stay Unsorted, and asserts ≥ 85% top-1 accuracy on non-`other` holdout items (≥ 82% on holdout2) and ≤ 35% of `other` items auto-assigned on both, with the installed groups only. On holdout3, with all groups present, it asserts ≥ 82% top-1 on non-`other` items and ≥ 70% on each on-demand label. The YouTube check asserts that no-topic videos land in `videos` at ≥ 70% and topic videos in their topic group at no less than the rate first measured (written in the test). It also checks that the owner's case (LangGraph YouTube tutorial and Claude Agent SDK docs) lands in AI & Agents. For model experiments, `HEAD_PATH` and `MODEL_PATH` point it at an alternative head and model directory.
`suspicious.test.ts` measures the content clues (`addressClue`, `titleClue`) from `src/background/suspicious.ts`. It runs them over every normal-browsing corpus row (fixture, both holdouts and the four training files) and requires that no row has both an ad-click address and a scam title, printing how many fire alone. It then runs them over `spam-fixture.json`, about 40 made-up pop-under, ad-click, gambling, fake-alert, fake-update and prize pages on invented domains, printing the share with both clues and with at least one, and requires at least one clue on 80% or more. The fixture is written from what such pages look like, not tuned to the rules. Run with `npx vitest run tests/quality/suspicious.test.ts --silent=false`.
