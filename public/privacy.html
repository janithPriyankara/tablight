Privacy policy

Tablight is built so that your browsing stays on your device. Nothing leaves it except the optional sync of tabs you save for later and of the examples you teach your groups, which goes through your own browser account, and the text you choose to look up with “Look it up”, described below.

Last updated 20 November 2026. Applies to the Tablight browser extension for Microsoft Edge and Google Chrome.

Who we are

Tablight is made by The Moonstone (Pvt) Ltd, Sanasuma, Wawlagala, Baddegama, Sri Lanka (“we”). For any privacy question or request, email priyankarajanith71@gmail.com.

We do not receive, collect or store any of your data. Everything described below stays on your device or in your own browser account, where we cannot see it.

When Tablight uses the network

Tablight makes no network requests, with one exception that you control: the “Look it up” button on Unsorted tabs. It has no servers, no accounts, no analytics, no tracking and no ads, and it asks for no host permissions. Site icons are read from the browser’s own icon cache, never downloaded from websites.

Look it up (optional)

On an Unsorted tab, “Look it up” can ask Wikidata, a public database run by the Wikimedia Foundation, what a name is, so the tab can be suggested a group. Nothing is sent until you press “Look up” in the dialog. The dialog first shows the exact text that would be sent, cleaned of email addresses, links, numbers and token-like strings, and you can edit it.

  • One request goes to Wikidata’s public search API. It carries only that text: no cookies, no referrer, no tab address, no account or device identifier.
  • Look it up is refused for tabs that are not ordinary web pages, local or private addresses, and sensitive tabs such as banking, payments, sign-in, medical, legal, salary or tax pages, and mail, calendar and document sites.
  • Answers are kept on your device (up to 500) so the same text is not asked twice. They are never synced, and “Delete all data” removes them.
  • Wikidata handles the request under the Wikimedia Foundation privacy policy; we cannot see it. Like any website, it can see your network address.
  • You can hide the button with Settings → “Show the Look it up button”. When it is hidden, or when you never press it, Tablight makes no network requests.

What Tablight cannot see

Tablight has no access to websites themselves. It cannot read your passwords, cookies, sign-ins, payment details, form entries, anything you type, or the contents of any page. It does not read your browsing history. All it receives from the browser is the title and address of each open tab.

When you install it, the browser describes the “tabs” permission as being able to read your browsing history, because tab addresses show which sites are open. Tablight uses them only to list and group your tabs on your device.

Grouping runs on your computer

Tabs are grouped by an AI model that ships inside the extension and runs on your device. It only uses each tab’s title and address. Page contents are never read.

The extension also carries a list of about 60,000 well-known names (films, series, games, music, anime, books, cars, sports, hardware), built from Wikidata (CC0) and read only on your device. It is checked only against the search words of a search-engine results page or the title of a Wikipedia page, never against ordinary page titles, and checking it makes no network request.

What is stored on your device

The list of your tabs (address, title, group, and whether it is open, paused, saved for later or not yet opened), your groups and what you taught them, saved tabs, saved sessions, settings, the saved answers of Look it up, the on-device model’s numeric summaries (embeddings) of tab titles and addresses, and a few local backup copies of your saved tabs. These are kept in the extension’s local storage. Private (incognito) windows are never recorded.

To spot tabs that open in the background and look like ads or scams, Tablight also keeps a few things in the browser’s session memory (chrome.storage.session): which site opened a tab, whether a window is a pop-up, the tabs you chose to Keep (so they are not flagged again), and whether the toolbar badge is currently showing its warning. This is kept only until the browser closes, and it is never sent anywhere.

Sync through your browser account (optional)

If sync is on (the default), tabs you save for later are synced through your browser’s own extension sync storage, which is tied to your Microsoft account in Edge or your Google account in Chrome. Only the address, a short title, the group name and the date are synced, with the record ids, timestamps and deletion markers needed to keep your devices in step.

With sync on, Tablight also syncs what you taught your groups, so your other devices group tabs the way you do: for each tab you moved into a group, or in a group you accepted, the text the on-device model read for it (its title, site and a few words from its address), the group’s name and when you taught it. Up to 50 examples per group, newest first. Other open tabs and saved sessions are never synced. You can turn sync off at any time in Settings.

Sync is provided by Microsoft or Google under their own privacy terms; we have no access to it.

Your choices and rights

  • See your data: everything Tablight keeps is visible in its dashboard, and Settings → Export backup saves a copy to your computer.
  • Delete your data: Settings → “Delete all data” deletes everything Tablight stored on this device and the synced copy of your saved tabs in your browser account, and turns sync off on this device. Uninstalling removes the data on this device. Use “Delete all data” on each of your devices to remove it everywhere.
  • Stop syncing: turn sync off in Settings. This stops syncing but keeps the copy already in your browser account until you use “Delete all data”.

Because we hold no personal data about you, there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on our side. If you have a question about your rights, including under Sri Lanka’s Personal Data Protection Act, No. 9 of 2022, or the EU/UK GDPR, email us.

Backups

Export saves a backup file to your computer. It contains your saved tabs, your groups and your saved sessions (the addresses and titles of the tabs in them). Import reads a backup file you choose. These files are only written or read locally, by you; keep them somewhere private.

Permissions

Tablight reads your tabs to list and group them; uses storage to keep its data, with unlimited local storage so large tab lists fit; uses alarms for auto-pause and hourly snapshots; uses an offscreen document to run the on-device model; shows itself in the browser’s side panel when you open it there; and uses the browser’s icon cache for site icons. The optional “tab groups” permission is only requested if you turn on mirroring to the browser’s tab groups.

Children

Tablight is a general-purpose tool. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If we change how Tablight handles data, we will update this page and the date above before the change ships, and describe it in the extension’s store listing.
